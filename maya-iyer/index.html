We're in a sleep crisis

Roughly one in three adults sleeps under seven hours a night, and most of them have stopped noticing.
We're scrolling later, waking earlier, and treating rest as the thing we do once everything else is finished.
The data is blunt: short sleep is now tied to heart disease, weight gain, anxiety and slower thinking.

But here's the good news:
sleep is trainable.

In the same way we learned to train strength and endurance, we can train rest - through timing, light, temperature and a handful of habits that compound.

Hi, I'm Maya Iyer and I'm proof that rest can be learned

People are often surprised to hear this, but for most of my twenties I was a terrible sleeper.

Through residency I ran on five hours, three coffees and the belief that rest was something I would earn later. My blood pressure, my memory and my mood all disagreed.

Over the past two decades I've studied sleep and recovery at two universities, run a sleep lab, and written six books (and counting) on what actually moves the needle.

Beneath the credentials is someone who had to learn all of it the slow way - through data, mistakes, and a lot of early nights.

I want the same thing for you, minus the twenty years.
